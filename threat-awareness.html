Our editorial approach

How we cover current events

We report on patterns and trends drawn from public reporting — never on individual victims, never with speculation about ongoing cases, and never implying a tragedy could have been prevented "if only." The goal is for you to understand a real category of risk well enough to make a calm, informed decision about your own preparedness.

Residential — Trend

Burglary rings hid spy cameras inside fake turf, then used fake deliveries to check if anyone was home

LA-area sheriff's officials described organized theft crews hiding phone-and-camera rigs — with extra batteries, wrapped in artificial turf — on properties to record residents' routines for days. The same crews reportedly used WiFi jammers to disable home security networks, then tested whether a house was occupied by leaving a fake food-delivery bag and ringing the doorbell before breaking in. Seven people were arrested.

Source: FOX 11 Los Angeles, May 21, 2026.

Commercial — Trend

Security teams fear their own IT admins more than outside hackers

A 2025 industry survey found 83% of security leaders rank IT administrators and engineers as the highest-risk insider group — ahead of third-party vendors (77%) and executives (64%). Separately, 68% believe more than 10% of their own workforce could become an insider threat "if sufficiently incentivized or under personal stress" — treated as a structural access issue, not a character issue.

Source: Cybersecurity Insiders, 2025 Insider Risk Report, Oct. 30, 2025.

Personal / Travel — Trend

Anyone can track a private jet in real time — legally — using data planes are required to broadcast

ADS-B transponders, required by the FAA on most aircraft for collision-avoidance safety, continuously broadcast unencrypted position, altitude, and tail-number data that hobbyist sites aggregate into live public flight trackers — no hacking involved, just a $30 receiver or a website. A 2024 federal reauthorization law now lets private aircraft owners petition to withhold registration data from public feeds, though the raw broadcast signal itself still can't be fully hidden.

Source: The War Zone, May 17, 2024 — the legal landscape has continued evolving through 2025–2026.

Entertainment — Trend

Every NFL stadium now verifies staff by selfie instead of a badge

The NFL rolled out facial-authentication technology league-wide across all 32 stadiums starting in the 2024 season, specifically to stop forged or borrowed credentials from granting access to secure areas like the locker room or press box. It applies to staff, vendors, and media — not fans — with the vendor claiming 99.7% accuracy.

Source: The Record (Recorded Future News), Aug. 1, 2024.
